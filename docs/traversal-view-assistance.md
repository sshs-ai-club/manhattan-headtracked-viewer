# Experimental traversal view assistance

Issue #21. **OFF by default each session.** Press Esc to open Settings. The separate panel at the upper left enables the experiment and adjusts look-up/down and corner-bank strength independently. Closing Settings resumes movement. Setting either strength to zero removes that cue; switching OFF eases to neutral and restores the original hierarchy. No calibration or preference file is changed.

## Cues and comfort boundaries

- Wall-running ascent above 2 m/s gradually looks up, reaching a target of 55 degrees at 14 m/s (full strength). Minimal-grip wall-anchor ascent uses the same cue when the rope is attached to a vertical surface, the anchor is more than 2 m higher and velocity rises above 2 m/s toward that anchor. This observes the winch ascent without forcing the existing WASD-dependent WallRunning state; roof/ceiling anchors, detached jumps and moving away are excluded.
- Airborne descent above 6 m/s gradually looks down, reaching a target of 35 degrees at 24 m/s. Grounding, ordinary ascent, pause, lost focus and flight mode return toward neutral.
- Horizontal travel above 3 m/s banks into a corner, bounded to 8 degrees at full strength. There is no automatic yaw or route following.
- Default strengths after opt-in are 60% pitch and 35% bank. The targets pass through two cascaded exponential filters: pitch frequency 0.8/s and bank 2/s. Exact sample integration avoids Euler overshoot. For arbitrary bounded target changes, each axis stays inside its target range; pitch rate is below 27 degrees/s and acceleration below 66 degrees/s²; bank below 12 degrees/s and 73 degrees/s². These are autonomous filter bounds, not measured comfort results or combined-axis limits; explicit resets and manual-pole clipping are excluded.
- Applied observer look from any input adapter suppresses assistance targets immediately and holds them neutral for 0.8 seconds after the last change. Existing motion decays smoothly rather than snapping. Manual look still moves immediately and owns the vertical poles. Clipping discards hidden pitch from both filter stages so moving away from a pole cannot reveal stored tilt and cancel the user’s look. This observes the applied view rotation rather than consuming the input source twice.
- Frame gaps above 100 ms discard motion cues and clear heading history; filter time advances by at most 100 ms. Teleports reset cues immediately. Explicit component destruction/disable restores the view immediately; the normal OFF control uses the smooth path.

## Tracking and ownership

The camera and the existing physical screen become children of an identity view pivot below the observer. Only that pivot rotates, before the pinned package's LateUpdate. The package still owns the tracked eye, camera pose and off-axis matrix. Screen-local eye XYZ, calibration, screen dimensions, physical window geometry and projection are unchanged by this rigid transform. Tracking OFF continues to freeze the head offset, not traversal. The feature does not read webcam data as gaze: the current tracker estimates eye position, not gaze direction.

The observer, body, input source, collision/constraint and forces are never changed. The existing camera-based aim ray naturally follows the assisted view, so enabling the view can change what the user chooses to aim at; this is not a claim of identical closed-loop player trajectories. Physics with identical supplied actions remains untouched.

The installer skips benchmarks, scripted/agent traversal and test processes so historical AI route comparisons remain unchanged. No scene, asmdef, package, firmware, v1 wire format or existing input/physics file is edited. Reparenting is refused if another owner has already changed the expected camera/screen hierarchy. Calibration changes while installed are retained on restoration.

## Follow-up: travel tangent, two gloves, and what is observable

The original experiment did **not** follow travel yaw and ordinary rising swings had no upward pitch cue. It was also OFF in the interactive minimal-grip launch. Therefore historical route passes did not demonstrate visible tangent-following camera motion.

The new optional tangent mode computes heading and elevation from actual body velocity in the observer frame. Airborne travel above 3 m/s follows the parabolic ascent/descent and horizontal corner heading, with independently rate/acceleration-limited pitch (40 deg/s, 100 deg/s²) and yaw (55 deg/s, 120 deg/s²). Nearly vertical travel retains heading. The yaw angle unwraps across ±180°. It does not rotate or steer the body. Manual observer look retains priority with the existing 0.8 s holdoff; pause, invalid telemetry, focus loss and grounding return toward neutral. Pitch poles still clip for manual control. These limits are mathematical constraints, not comfort certification.

Explicitly enable using Settings or `--traversal-view`; plain launch is still OFF. Tangent is selected when assistance is enabled, and its pitch replaces rather than stacks with the legacy fall/climb cue. Disable tangent to use the legacy pitch strength. Bank stays independently adjustable. The common screen/camera pivot preserves the physical-screen eye relation. Positional head tracking remains distinct from gaze or tracked head orientation; no gaze is inferred from eye XYZ.

### Proposed dual-glove boundary (not implemented hardware support)

Each left/right glove needs its own device identity, timestamp/sequence, press/hold/release state, calibration and pose quality. Duplicate or old samples must not create new presses, one hand disconnect must not reuse the other's pose, and reconnection must require released state before firing. Two valid held grips may request the Q-like two-anchor charge; each anchor must originate from its own hand ray. The current single `yaw,pitch,fire` v1 protocol does not supply this, and is not silently reinterpreted here.

A glove direction is relative to a calibrated physical body/screen reference, **not** the auto-rotating view pivot. Otherwise a stationary real hand would silently change direction as the camera follows a swing. The game's body/travel reference can turn, but this intentional mapping must be separate from the view-only comfort transform. Physical hand position must not be fabricated from acceleration double-integration. A fixed left/right virtual emission origin is an explicitly approximate fallback, not tracked hand position.

An accelerometer observes specific force, mixing gravity and translational acceleration. At rest, rotation around gravity leaves the reading unchanged, so absolute yaw is unobservable. During a vigorous gesture even tilt is ambiguous without separating motion from gravity. Adding a second independent accelerometer glove does not fix each glove's yaw. Analog Devices documents the static-gravity assumption and acceleration interference in [AN-1057](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/app-notes/an-1057.html).

For unrestricted directional shooting, the proposed practical minimum is one accelerometer+gyroscope IMU per glove, calibrated initial heading and explicit recentering; yaw still drifts without a reliable external heading reference. A magnetometer or optical reference can constrain heading but must be validated for indoor interference/occlusion. With accelerometers **only**, use an honestly labeled tilt/gesture controller with a fixed calibrated heading or target-sector selection, not a claimed measured 3D aim. This restriction cannot be corrected away by software.

No-mouse acceptance must use independent left/right pose streams with noise, drift, latency, dropouts, out-of-order data and changed calibration; camera follows actual motion and cannot feed back into the glove ray. Report collisions and route completion separately, and include both-hand and single-hand release, recapture, corners and changing swing pitch. Existing exact-angle AI fixtures are not hardware-only evidence. Real glove latency, human comfort, rendered tangent behavior, and no-mouse full-city play remain untested.
